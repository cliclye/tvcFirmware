EASYTVC FLIGHT COMPUTER + SOFTWARE: SPECS FOR THE CAD DESIGNER
Rocket: TVCRocketV5.2 (finless, thrust-vector controlled)   Firmware 0.5.0   2026-11-03

How to read this
- Positions are mm from the NOSE TIP along the rocket axis unless stated.
  (In the Fusion CAD z is up from the motor-tube bottom: tip distance = 1060 - z.)
- [VERIFY] = not measured on the real part yet. Measure before final CAD.
- Firmware body axes: +Z = toward the nose.


==========================================================================================
1. THE ROCKET IN ONE PARAGRAPH
==========================================================================================
A 3-inch (79 mm) finless rocket that stays upright only because a flight computer steers the
motor with a 2-axis gimbal (thrust vector control, TVC). There are no fins: aerodynamically it is
unstable on purpose (CP ahead of CG), so the TVC must work from the moment of launch. The
parachute is deployed by the flight computer (currently planned: pyro ejection charge on P1).

Key numbers (V5.2, as entered in Studio -> Rocket setup; weigh real parts to confirm)
  Overall length (tip -> nozzle exit)   1062 mm
  Body tube                             kraft 3": OD 76.2 / ID 74.4 mm (upper 413 + lower 457 mm, coupler)
  Nose                                  tangent ogive 180 mm + 50 mm shoulder (OD 76.0)
  Liftoff mass (Estes F15-0)            949 g   (burnout 889 g)          [VERIFY by weighing]
  CG                                    614 mm at liftoff, 588 mm at burnout
  CP (finless)                          84 mm  (about -6.6 calibers: unstable, by design)
  Gimbal pivot                          1003 mm  -> gimbal arm (pivot to CG) 388 mm
  Motor                                 Estes F15-0, 29 x 114 mm, 102 g
  Simulated flight                      apogee ~56-66 m at ~5.2 s (1.0 deg boost tilt, nominal)
  MASS WARNING: Estes rates the F15-0 for 539 g max. At 949 g the rocket leaves slowly.
                Every gram saved in the airframe helps.

3. HOW THE BOARD MUST BE MOUNTED (this matters to the software)
==========================================================================================
Orientation (the firmware is configured for exactly this: EASYTVC_NOSE_AXIS = POS_Y)
  - Board stands VERTICALLY on the FC sled, in a plane through the rocket's axis.
  - Pyro / SW screw terminals toward the NOSE; XT30 toward the TAIL.
  - Component side faces the front window (CAD +Y).
  - Board long edge parallel to the rocket axis to within ~1 deg.
    (A tilted board reads as a tilted rocket. Small errors can be trimmed, large ones can't.)
  - If the board ends up mounted any other way, tell the software side: it's a one-line
    change, but it must be known before flight.

Position
  - IMU on (or very near) the rocket's centre line. V5.2: board 764-844 mm, IMU chip at
    ~818 mm, about 204 mm behind the CG (the FC moved to the tail end in V4/V5). The sim models
    this and passes; the accelerometer is only used on the pad, so the risk is small.
  - Mount RIGID. No foam or rubber isolation: a board that wobbles on its mount makes the
    attitude estimate wrong. Screws into a stiff sled.
  - Keep it away from the motor heat and from the LiPo's magnetic/thermal mess where you can.

Firmware body frame with this mounting (for anyone checking the CAD against logs)
  body Z = sensor +Y = nose
  body X = sensor +Z = CAD +Y
  body Y = sensor +X = CAD -X

4. WIRING MAP (current setup in Core/Inc/vehicle_config.h)
==========================================================================================
  Servo header S1  ->  TVC servo, firmware "servo B" = CAD "TVC Servo A" (INNER gimbal)
  Servo header S2  ->  TVC servo, firmware "servo A" = CAD "TVC Servo B" (OUTER gimbal)
  Servo header S3  ->  free (was the parachute-release servo; not used with pyro)
  Servo header S4  ->  free
  Pyro P1          ->  parachute ejection charge (e-match)
  Pyro P2          ->  NEVER CONNECT
  Pyro P3          ->  free (could be a second/backup charge later)
  SW               ->  pyro arming switch (see section 7)                      [VERIFY]
  7-pin connector  ->  XIAO ESP32-C3 radio: 5V-5V, GND-GND, TX2 -> XIAO D2 (GPIO4),
                       RX2 -> XIAO D3 (GPIO5). Never use XIAO pins D6/D7.
  XT30             ->  2S LiPo
  All of these can be re-assigned in software (Studio -> Rocket setup -> Board outputs), but
  the CAD wire routing should follow this map.

Wire routing
  - Servo leads run aft from the sled through the empty tube section (678-932 mm) to the
    gimbal servos at ~976 and ~1027 mm. Leave a channel/clip path; keep them off the motor.
  - Pyro leads from P1 forward through the recovery bulkhead to the charge (section 7).
  - V5.2: servo leads are short, ~100-200 mm from the gimbal through the baffle to the board's
    bottom-edge headers. No long extensions needed.

5. TVC GIMBAL / MOTOR MOUNT (what the software expects)
==========================================================================================
  Gimbal design           K-9 V8: outer ring bolted to the airframe (2x M3x8), inner gimbal on
                          M3x12 pivots, motor tube on M3x6 pivots (V5 outer ring: M3x6, thinner
                          trimmed ring; M3x8 also fine)
  Pivot location          1003 mm from tip; 59.8 mm above the nozzle exit
  Motor sleeve            OD 31.4 mm printed (the 31.8 one jammed), or cardboard
  TVC servos              TowerPro MG90D (metal gear, ~13 g). Two, one per axis.
  Linkage                 servo horn hole 9 mm, pushrod lever ~42 mm -> ratio ~0.214,
                          1 mm music wire with Z-bends
  Commanded travel        servo pulses 1250 / 1500 / 1750 us (min / centre / max)
                          = about +-22.5 deg of horn = about +-4.7 deg of motor
  Mechanical clearance    airframe cut-outs must clear +-12 deg of motor tilt in BOTH axes
                          without binding (the simulator uses +-12 deg as the hard stop)
  Servo update rate       50 Hz (standard servo signal)
  Requirements
    - Zero play / slop at centre is the single most important thing for TVC accuracy.
    - "Straight" must be repeatable: the software can trim each axis by up to +-100 us
      (about +-2 deg of motor) over the radio, but the mount should be straight within
      ~1 deg as built.
    - Each servo moves only its own axis (no coupling between the two).
    - Blast baffle (solid, at ~932-936 mm) keeps motor gas off the servos and wiring.
    - Use PETG/ASA (not PLA) for anything near the motor.

6. RADIO (WIRELESS TELEMETRY / BLUETOOTH)
==========================================================================================
  Module          Seeed XIAO ESP32-C3 running DroneBridge firmware (21 x 17.8 mm)  [VERIFY]
  Setup           single board: the XIAO runs DroneBridge as a Wi-Fi access point and the
                  Mac joins its Wi-Fi directly (no ground unit; up to ~150 m). An ESP-NOW
                  ground unit (~1 km) may be added later; nothing in the rocket changes for it
  Placement
    - Close to the tube wall, away from the LiPo, the motor and any carbon fibre or metal.
      Blue Tube is radio-transparent; carbon fibre and foil are NOT.
    - Use the XIAO's external antenna; route it along the inside of the tube wall, pointing
      along the rocket axis, not bundled with servo or power wires.
    - Secure it: it must not move at launch. Wire it to the 7-pin connector (section 4).
  Power           from the board's 5 V (draws up to ~0.3 A while transmitting)


==========================================================================================
7. RECOVERY: PYRO EJECTION CHARGE (current plan) -> CAD REQUIREMENTS
==========================================================================================
How the software fires it
  - Never before launch is detected. Never after an unexpected reboot.
  - Fires ONCE at: detected apogee (or, if set, when the rocket comes back down through a
    chosen height), OR at the backup timer (currently 5.8 s after launch), whichever is first.
  - Output on P1 for 1.0 s.
  - The pyro outputs are LOCKED in the firmware until the board's pyro channels have passed a
    bench test with a test load (LED / resistor). The airframe should be designed for pyro now.

What the airframe needs for a charge
  1. A charge holder ("charge well" / cup) on the parachute side of the recovery bulkhead
     (~460 mm), sized for an e-match + black powder. Amount is set by a ground ejection test,
     not by design; leave room for ~0.5-1.5 g of BP in a cup with a removable cap. [designer]
  2. A sealed pass-through for the two e-match wires through the recovery bulkhead (epoxy or a
     small terminal block). The avionics bay must be GAS-TIGHT on the charge side: ejection gas
     must not reach the board or the barometer.
  3. Nose cone retention that holds through boost and coast but lets go on the charge: friction
     fit or 2x nylon shear pins (e.g. 2-56). The current spring/piston/latch-servo release is no
     longer needed with pyro (the latch servo at 233-265 mm can be removed); keep the piston if
     it helps protect the chute.                                              [designer decision]
  4. Flame/heat protection for the chute: Nomex blanket or wadding between charge and chute.
  5. Room to connect the e-match LAST, at the pad (the charge bay must be reachable after the
     rocket is otherwise assembled), or a connector at the bulkhead.
  6. Pyro arming: a way to keep the pyro circuit off until the rocket is on the pad. Recommended:
     an external switch (or screw switch / pull-pin) reachable from outside the tube, wired to
     the board's SW terminal or in series with the charge.                     [VERIFY SW function]
  7. Shock cord anchored to the recovery bulkhead U-bolt (M4), Kevlar, quick links.

  Parachute: Estes 30" nylon (sim lands ~6.6 m/s; a 36" chute would land under 5 m/s).


==========================================================================================
8. BAROMETER VENTING (needed for apogee / altitude-based deployment)
==========================================================================================
  - The board's bay needs static-port holes to the outside air: 1-3 holes, ~1 mm each, evenly
    around the tube, in the avionics bay section.
  - NOT near the nose/body joint or any shoulder, NOT in line with the motor exhaust, and away
    from the window edge (smooth airflow over the holes).
  - The bay must otherwise be sealed: no leaks to the charge/chute bay (ejection pressure would
    look like a false altitude), and no big openings that let the airflow pump the bay.
  - Light foam over the barometer chip is OK (keeps light and gusts off it); do not seal it.

9. ACCESS THE ROCKET MUST GIVE (holes, hatches, windows)
==========================================================================================
  - Power: the XT30 unplug is the power switch, so either a hatch to reach it or an external
    power switch in the battery lead.
  - USB-C port: to install firmware without disassembly (hole in the tube at the port).
  - BOOT button: needed together with USB-C to install firmware.             [VERIFY location]
  - microSD card: removable after a flight to read the full log (a hatch or easy sled pull-out).
  - Status LED visible from outside (window): solid green = ARMED (hands off), red = do not
    launch, blue blink = alive/recording.
  - Buzzer audible (an open path / small hole); it beeps a locator pattern after landing.
  - Pyro arming switch reachable on the pad (section 7, item 6).
  - Battery: 2S LiPo 300-450 mAh, XT30, ~20-31 g, on its own sled (current CAD: 468-550 mm,
    V5.2 battery sled centre ~687 mm). Must be removable for charging.

10. HOW THE SOFTWARE WORKS (so the design makes sense)
==========================================================================================
Main loop: 200 times per second (every 5 ms). Every loop it reads the IMU, updates the
attitude estimate, runs the flight state machine, computes the TVC command, drives the servos
and pyro outputs, logs to SD and sends telemetry. Barometer read at 50 Hz.

Flight states
  IDLE     on the pad, powered. Calibrates the gyro while still.
  ARMED    armed itself after standing still and vertical (within 10 deg) for 3 s.
           Solid green LED + chirp every second. Tipped past 20 deg -> back to IDLE.
           (Needs a working SD card.)
  BOOST    launch detected: acceleration above 1.8 g for 40 ms. TVC starts 80 ms later.
  COAST    motor burnt out. TVC off, gimbal centred.
  APOGEE   vertical speed reaches zero (or the backup timer) -> parachute deployment.
  DESCENT  under the chute. Logging continues.
  LANDED   on the ground: locator beeps.
  FAULT    something failed for real (e.g. tilted past 25 deg in boost, sensors lost for
           more than 100 ms): TVC gives up and centres; the parachute backup timer still runs.

TVC control (finless stability)
  - Attitude: quaternion estimate from the gyro, corrected by the accelerometer only while on
    the pad (during flight the motor's push hides gravity).
  - Tilt error = angle of the nose away from vertical, about body X and Y.
  - Two PID loops (V5.2 gains P 16, I 2.2, D 1.5, D-filter 20 Hz, slew limit 28 travel/s)
    turn tilt into a gimbal command, which becomes servo pulses (1250-1750 us).
  - Roll compensation: a finless rocket rolls; the software rotates the commands so the motor
    still pushes the right way while the body spins (tested to 2000 deg/s in simulation).
  - Glitch tolerance: a missed sensor reading holds the last command instead of giving up.
  - Mechanical implication: the controller assumes the servo/gimbal respond in ~15 ms and the
    linkage is linear and slop-free. Slop and a slow or flexible linkage are the biggest risks.

Recovery: see section 7 (pyro). The software can also drive a release servo instead (on any free
servo header) if the design ever goes back to a servo latch.

Data and ground link
  - SD card logs at 100 Hz from arming to landing (10 Hz on the pad): attitude, rates, accel,
    altitude, servo pulses, state, events. Readable in Studio, with a 3D replay.
  - Radio telemetry at 20 Hz in flight (state, altitude, speed, tilt, gimbal, battery,
    barometer, parachute countdown). Losing the radio has NO effect on the flight.
  - On the pad only (IDLE, not armed), Studio can: read the status/config, download logs, move
    and align the gimbal, tune gains and the TVC centre, test the pyro channels with a test
    load, and run an LED/buzzer check. Nothing can be commanded over the radio in flight.

Firmware images
  bench        never arms; the gimbal follows the board by hand (for servo/alignment checks)
  flight       arms on the pad and flies
  sensortest   checks every sensor and logs raw readings
  pyrobench    never arms; pyro outputs live for the pyro test with a test load ONLY.
               Never put this one in a rocket (fast red blink + chirp every 5 s).
